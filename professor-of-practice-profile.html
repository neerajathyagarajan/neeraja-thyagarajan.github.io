Leading Transformation, Building Effective Teams and Creating a Learning Culture

Professional contribution

My career in Quality Engineering leadership at Oracle has involved the interconnected challenges of people, technology, processes, and delivery. This experience offers students practical lessons in how to lead transformation: establish a clear purpose, develop capabilities, build trust, encourage experimentation, and translate collective effort into measurable outcomes.

As a Professor of Practice, I would bring this perspective to engineering, technology management, and interdisciplinary programs. My contribution would combine industry experience with a focus on the human and organizational capabilities that make change effective.

Teaching philosophy

I believe effective transformation begins with people understanding the purpose of change and having the confidence, skills, and support to contribute to it.

My teaching would help students become adaptable professionals who can learn continuously, question assumptions, collaborate across differences, and take responsibility for outcomes. I would encourage them to recognize individual strengths, seek complementary perspectives, and approach unfamiliar challenges with curiosity and discipline.

Through practical assignments and reflection, students would explore how leaders create environments where people can experiment, discuss setbacks openly, and improve together. Resilience would be taught through both individual adaptability and team practices such as clear priorities, shared ownership, and mutual support.

Teaching methods

  • Transformation case studies: Examine industry examples to understand the decisions, team dynamics, adoption challenges, and conditions behind successful outcomes.
  • Team-based projects: Ask students to address a practical problem, allocate work according to strengths, and reflect on how their collaboration evolved.
  • Experimentation labs: Have students test an idea, gather evidence, review results, and recommend whether to refine, expand, or stop the approach.
  • Leadership simulations: Explore situations involving resistance to change, unclear ownership, competing priorities, and disagreement across teams.
  • Mentoring and reflective practice: Help students recognize their strengths, respond constructively to feedback, and develop a personal learning plan.
  • Technical applications: Use Quality Engineering, automation, AI-assisted testing, and release governance as concrete settings in which these broader principles can be applied.

Contribution to academia

As a Professor of Practice, I could:

  • Develop or co-teach modules on transformation leadership, team effectiveness, continuous learning, and technology adoption.
  • Mentor interdisciplinary capstone teams on both their proposed solutions and how they organize, collaborate, and execute.
  • Conduct workshops on resilience, experimentation, feedback, collaboration, and leading through uncertainty.
  • Help students connect technical decisions with business objectives, stakeholder needs, and organizational realities.
  • Support faculty in designing assignments that assess teamwork, judgment, adaptability, and execution alongside technical competence.
  • Provide career mentoring on professional communication, personal strengths, learning agility, and progression into leadership.
  • Contribute industry perspectives to curriculum development and discussions on emerging workplace capabilities.
  • Offer specialist teaching in Quality Engineering, automation modernization, and enterprise delivery governance.

Intended student impact

My aim is to help students become professionals who can contribute effectively to change and eventually lead it. They should be able to build trust, draw on one another’s strengths, learn through experimentation, and deliver results with shared accountability.

The lasting contribution I would seek to make is a combination of practical judgment, collaborative leadership, and the capacity to keep learning as technologies and workplaces evolve.
